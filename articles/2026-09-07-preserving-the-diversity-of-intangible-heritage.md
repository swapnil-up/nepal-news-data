summary: The article critiques Nepal’s newly drafted heritage Bill, arguing that while it acknowledges the importance of safeguarding intangible cultural heritage, it falls short by lacking an inclusive governing board. The author stresses that statutory language alone cannot ensure the protection of diverse traditions, languages, and practices. Instead, a representative board comprising community leaders, scholars, and cultural practitioners is essential for genuine preservation. The piece calls on policymakers to broaden participation and align the Bill with UNESCO’s guidelines, warning that without such inclusivity, Nepal risks losing its rich, living heritage.
