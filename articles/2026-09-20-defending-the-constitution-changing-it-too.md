summary: On Constitution Day, Nepal marked the adoption of the 2015 charter with official ceremonies and speeches praising its role in ending monarchy and establishing federalism. However, journalists and civil‑society leaders highlighted that the same document leaves many groups—particularly Madhesis, Dalits, women and other minorities—still facing exclusion from political representation, land rights and public services. Critics argued that while the constitution enshrines fundamental rights, its implementation mechanisms are weak and the federal boundaries favour dominant groups. The government pledged to address these gaps through amendments and inclusive policies, but opposition parties warned that without genuine power‑sharing the promise of a unified, democratic Nepal remains unfulfilled.
