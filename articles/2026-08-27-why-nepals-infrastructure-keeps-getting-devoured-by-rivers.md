summary: The article examines why Nepal’s roads, bridges and other infrastructure are repeatedly destroyed by river floods. It argues that outdated planning assumes a stable, calm river system, ignoring the intensified monsoon rains and glacial melt driven by climate change. Weak design standards, insufficient drainage, and a lack of coordinated investment among ministries leave critical assets vulnerable. The piece calls for a shift toward resilient, flood‑aware engineering, better data sharing, and stronger political will to prioritize adaptation before the next disaster strikes.
