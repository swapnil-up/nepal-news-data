summary: The article examines why Nepal lacks a legal framework to safeguard Yomari, a beloved Newari sweet that symbolizes the nation’s intangible cultural heritage. It outlines the gaps in existing heritage laws, the absence of specific protection for traditional foods, and the challenges of recognizing such items under UNESCO conventions. Experts and community leaders argue that legislation, documentation, and public awareness are needed to prevent commercial exploitation and cultural erosion. The piece calls for coordinated action among the Ministry of Culture, Tourism and Civil Aviation, local cultural bodies, and policymakers to establish protective measures that honor Nepal’s culinary legacy.
