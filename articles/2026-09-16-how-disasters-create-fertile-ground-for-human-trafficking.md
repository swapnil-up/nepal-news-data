summary: The article examines how natural and man‑made disasters in Nepal create conditions that traffickers exploit. It outlines how displacement, loss of livelihoods, weakened law enforcement, and disrupted community networks leave victims—especially women and children—vulnerable to being lured or forced into labor and sexual exploitation. The piece highlights recent earthquakes and floods as case studies, noting spikes in trafficking reports following each event. It also discusses the role of NGOs, government agencies, and international bodies in identifying and rescuing victims, while stressing the need for stronger disaster‑response frameworks that integrate anti‑trafficking measures. The author calls for coordinated policy reforms, better data collection, and community awareness to prevent traffickers from turning disaster‑stricken areas into fertile ground for their crimes.
