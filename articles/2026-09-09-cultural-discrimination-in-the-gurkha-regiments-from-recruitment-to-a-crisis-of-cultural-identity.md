summary: The article examines the historical and ongoing cultural discrimination faced by Gurkha soldiers within British regiments. Beginning with recruitment after the 1816 Treaty of Sugauli, the British favored certain Nepali ethnic groups such as the Gurungs and Magars, marginalizing others. Over time, these practices have contributed to a crisis of cultural identity among Gurkha veterans, who feel their traditions and community bonds are eroded by institutional biases. The piece highlights personal testimonies, policy gaps, and calls for greater recognition of the diverse Nepali communities that serve in the Gurkha regiments.
