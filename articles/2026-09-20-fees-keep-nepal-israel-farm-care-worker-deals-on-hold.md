summary: Negotiations on Nepal‑Israel agreements to send Nepali farm and care workers to Israel have stalled because of a dispute over recruitment fees. Israeli authorities argue that private recruitment agencies have the legal right to charge workers for placement, while the Nepali government insists that recruitment should be free or at least worker‑friendly, warning that high fees exploit migrants. The deadlock threatens to delay the deployment of thousands of Nepali workers seeking higher wages abroad and raises concerns about labor rights, remittances, and bilateral ties.
