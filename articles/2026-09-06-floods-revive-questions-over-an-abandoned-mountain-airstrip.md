summary: Recent floods in the Langtang region have reignited debate over the abandoned Langtang Airport, a high‑altitude airstrip at 3,658 metres. While the runway has been out of service for years, the disaster highlighted its potential role in delivering relief supplies and evacuating victims from remote mountain communities. Government officials, tourism authorities, and local residents are weighing the costs of refurbishing the airstrip against safety concerns and environmental impact. Proponents argue that a functional airport could boost tourism and improve emergency response, while critics cite the high maintenance expenses and the risk of triggering further ecological disturbance. The discussion comes amid broader national conversations on infrastructure resilience and disaster preparedness in Nepal’s mountainous zones.
