summary: After years of chronic load‑shedding, Nepal announced an end to daily power cuts, crediting reforms in the Nepal Electricity Authority and stronger political will. However, the recent drop in river levels—especially in the Koshi and Trishuli basins—has sharply reduced hydro‑electric generation, the backbone of the country’s grid. With reservoirs low and monsoon rains delayed, the government faces renewed shortages, prompting emergency imports and calls for diversified energy sources. Energy Minister Shishir Khanal warned that without sustained water flow, the gains of better governance could be undone, while Prime Minister Pushpa Kamal Dahal urged accelerated investment in solar and wind projects. The article highlights the fragile balance between governance reforms and Nepal’s reliance on seasonal river water for power.
