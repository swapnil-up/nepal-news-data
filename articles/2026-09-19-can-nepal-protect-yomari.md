summary: The article examines why Nepal lacks a legal framework to protect Yomari, the iconic Newari sweet that symbolizes the nation’s intangible cultural heritage. Despite its recognition by UNESCO and its deep roots in Nepali identity, there is no specific legislation granting the state ownership or protection rights over Yomari. Experts argue that a dedicated heritage law, stronger enforcement mechanisms, and community involvement are essential to safeguard the tradition from commercialization and loss. The piece highlights ongoing discussions within the Ministry of Culture, Tourism and Civil Aviation, calls for collaboration with UNESCO, and urges civil society groups to push for policy reforms that would formally recognize and protect Yomari as a national treasure.
